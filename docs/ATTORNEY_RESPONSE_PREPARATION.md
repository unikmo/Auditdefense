# Attorney response preparation

## Purpose

Give counsel a source-linked claim and policy record before a response is drafted. The system organizes facts, evidence, policy versions, open questions, and historical decisions. The attorney selects arguments and controls all legal analysis and final language.

## Claim response record

For each payer claim or line, preserve:
- payer claim identifier and source row
- product / line of business
- date of service and original payment date
- billed amount, paid amount, direct or extrapolated amount, and demand stage
- billing, rendering, supervising, and contracting provider roles / identifiers
- payer allegation and exact source locator
- provider response, evidence artifact, source location, and whether payer receipt is verified
- applicable authority and policy version with effective dates, jurisdiction, source, locator, and verification status
- provider statement vs source-supported fact vs disputed item vs attorney-verified item
- attorney disposition and revision history

Do not infer claim-level evidence or policy applicability from aggregate counts. If line-level sources are absent, identify the gap and keep the result at aggregate level.

## Draft consistency screen

The current browser-side screen flags a limited set of configured issues: arithmetic inconsistency, conflated demand stages, product-line mixing, contradictory review-stage descriptions, and predictions of court outcomes. It is not an AI legal reviewer and a clean screen is not a finding of accuracy or completeness.

A full response should be assembled from one versioned fact ledger. Each factual sentence should link to source documents. Calculated totals should be computed from claim rows and display their inputs. Any changed number or unsupported assertion must be flagged for attorney review.

## Historical decisions

Show references only when issue tags match. For each reference display jurisdiction, precedential status, procedural posture, documented party positions where the opinion reports them, disposition, holding, source locator, factual similarities, and limits. Distinguish arguments made by a party from arguments adopted by the court. Do not infer a lawyer's strategy where the record does not document it. A historical decision is not a prediction or recommendation.

## Attorney control

The tool may identify issues, gaps, source conflicts, and potentially relevant historical material. It must not rank defenses, select strategy, state a legal conclusion, or predict an outcome. Counsel reviews the source record and approves any final response.

The consistency screen also flags categorical claims that clinical review legally admits coverage or prevents an enrollment-based recovery, and statements assigning enrollment responsibility without an identified DOS-effective rule, contract term, role, or source. These flags prompt review; they do not assess legal sufficiency or choose an argument.

The reference system reports the holding, case posture, parties' arguments when the opinion records them, why a case is tagged to an issue, and limits on comparison. An issue tag is a research lead, not a finding that the court decided the same issue or that the case predicts an outcome. If the source record does not describe attorney strategy, the system says so rather than inventing one.
