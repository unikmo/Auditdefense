ABA INSURANCE AUDIT

Trace every ABA claim from billed units back to the people and records that support it.

ABA reviews can involve technician notes, supervising clinician documentation, assessments, treatment plans, authorization periods, credentials and enrollment. Each issue should remain tied to its own claim line and date of service.

Organize the audit →
For each affected line, identify
  • CPT code, modifier and units;
  • date, time and place of service;
  • rendering technician or clinician;
  • supervising provider where applicable;
  • session record and active plan; and
  • authorization and enrollment periods.

Start with the payer’s exact allegation

“Documentation does not support the service” can describe different alleged defects: no note located, missing signature, incomplete fields, plan timing, unsupported units, provider identity or an enrollment issue. Preserve the reviewer’s wording and assign a separate issue category rather than rewriting every result as a generic denial.

AuditDefend identifies factual gaps and conflicts. It does not decide medical necessity, compliance, repayment liability or the legal sufficiency of a response.

ABA evidence map

Assessment and plan

Assessment date, author, signature, treatment goals, service period and later plan updates.

Direct-service record

Technician or clinician, date, start and end time, units, service code, note and signature.

Supervision record

Supervisor identity, relationship to the rendering provider, supervision date and supporting record.

Administrative support

Authorization, credentials, group affiliation, billing/rendering identifiers and enrollment effective dates.

How to verify a worksheet finding

Select the affected claim line.Confirm the member-safe identifier, date of service, code, units and rendering provider.
Preserve the payer’s stated reason.Do not substitute a broader label for the actual line-level decision.
Locate the contemporaneous record.Identify the source system and version rather than recreating missing documentation.
Record the comparison.Mark what appears present, absent or inconsistent and reserve conclusions for professional and attorney review.

Why issue separation matters

A claim may have clinical documentation that supports the delivered service while the payer separately alleges an enrollment, credentialing or authorization defect. Conversely, enrollment evidence does not cure a missing clinical record. AuditDefend preserves both tracks so the provider and counsel can see exactly what evidence addresses—and what it does not.
