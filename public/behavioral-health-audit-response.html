BEHAVIORAL HEALTH INSURANCE AUDIT

Connect each billed service to the record the reviewer says is missing.

Behavioral-health audits often spread evidence across an EHR, billing system, scanned files, authorization portal and provider records. A defensible workspace begins by identifying where the original source is held.

Organize the audit →
Common evidence sources
  • session or progress notes;
  • assessments and treatment plans;
  • signatures and attestations;
  • authorizations and utilization reviews;
  • supervision or rendering-provider records; and
  • billing, credentialing and enrollment systems.

Why a document inventory comes before a rebuttal

A finding such as “missing signature” or “missing treatment plan” is a statement about what the reviewer considered. The provider first needs to identify the exact document version, source system, service period and prior submission. If a signed record exists, the workspace should capture that factual conflict and preserve the source. It should not automatically conclude that the record is legally sufficient or that the payer must reverse its decision.

Clinical adequacy, contract interpretation, response strategy and legal effect remain subject to qualified professional and attorney review.

Build the record in layers

Claim layer

Date of service, procedure code, units, rendering provider, billed amount and paid amount.

Clinical layer

Assessment, active plan, session record, progress review and discharge or transition material.

Administrative layer

Authorization, eligibility, credentialing, network participation and provider enrollment.

Review layer

Initial decision, rebuttal evidence, later decision and the reviewer’s stated remaining issue.

Typical factual comparisons

  • Signature finding: locate the signed source record, signer identity, date and version history.
  • Treatment-plan finding: map the plan’s service period to the affected dates of service.
  • Missing-note finding: identify the record system, note date, service code and prior production history.
  • Supervision finding: connect the rendering individual, supervisor, supervision record and applicable period.
  • Authorization finding: compare approved service, dates and units with the billed claim line.
  • Enrollment finding: keep billing and rendering identifiers separate and test status on the exact date of service.

Provider and attorney roles remain distinct

The provider identifies record locations and supplies permitted source material. AuditDefend maps evidence to affected claims and payer findings. The attorney then reviews the factual comparison, controlling sources and available response options. Counsel work product is private by default, and an invitation alone does not create an engagement or expose documents.
