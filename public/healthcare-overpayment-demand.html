HEALTHCARE OVERPAYMENT DEMAND

The amount demanded is a claim to reconcile—not a number to repeat blindly.

An insurer’s letter may combine sampled claims, component totals, prior payments, offsets or extrapolated amounts. Each layer needs its own source and calculation trail.

Organize the demand →
Capture these figures separately
  • total amount asserted;
  • amount represented in the supplied claim file;
  • sampled billed and paid dollars;
  • refunds, offsets or recoveries already taken; and
  • any extrapolation population and method.

What is a healthcare overpayment demand?

It is a payer’s request to return amounts the payer says were not payable under an identified contract, policy, program rule or claim requirement. The letter may follow a desk review, documentation request, SIU review, program-integrity audit or post-payment audit. The stated amount should be recorded as asserted until its components and sources are reconciled.

Whether an amount is legally recoverable, whether a response is required and which deadline controls are questions for qualified counsel reviewing the governing documents.

Reconcile the demand in layers

Letter total

The exact figure stated in the current demand, with the letter date and version.

Claim-file total

The billed and paid amounts that can be reproduced from the supplied claim rows.

Finding total

Amounts tied to each documentation, enrollment, coding or authorization category.

Recovery activity

Refunds, recoupments, offsets and corrected claims that may affect the remaining balance.

Questions the record should answer

  • Does every demanded dollar map to an identified claim and payment?
  • Are the date-of-service and payment periods the same?
  • Are any claim lines duplicated across worksheets or issue categories?
  • Does a percentage describe claim count, paid dollars or an extrapolation?
  • Were amounts already refunded, voided, corrected or offset?
  • Does the payer distinguish sampled dollars from the broader asserted balance?

Do not confuse error rate with financial exposure

A claim-count error rate does not automatically equal the percentage of paid dollars at issue. A sample with many low-dollar findings can produce a high count rate and a different paid-dollar rate. AuditDefend preserves both calculations and labels the payer’s broader figure separately so counsel can review the methodology without inheriting an unsupported calculation.

Build the evidence and response record

Preserve every demand version.Store the letter date, attachments, delivery evidence and stated response date.
Normalize the claims.Use stable claim identifiers, dates of service, codes, units, billed amounts and paid amounts.
Map findings to money.Keep each issue family separate and identify overlapping allegations rather than double-counting them.
Record unresolved math.If the component totals do not reproduce the demand, show the difference instead of forcing the numbers to agree.
