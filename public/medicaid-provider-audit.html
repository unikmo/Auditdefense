MEDICAID PROVIDER AUDIT

Match every date of service to the correct program, provider and rule.

Medicaid audits can involve state fee-for-service rules, managed-care requirements, provider enrollment, billing and rendering identifiers, documentation and authorization. The applicable source and effective date matter.

  • policy version cited by the reviewer.

Why Medicaid audit records need date-specific review

Medicaid is administered through state programs within federal requirements, and managed-care organizations may apply program and contract requirements to paid claims. A current enrollment status or current manual does not by itself establish the status or rule that applied on an earlier date of service. The record should preserve the source, version and effective period used for each finding.

AuditDefend’s source library is a research and organization aid. It does not establish that a rule controls a claim; counsel verifies the governing authority and its application.

Separate the major review tracks

Program and payer

State Medicaid fee-for-service, managed care plan, waiver or other program context.

Provider enrollment

Billing and rendering NPI, Medicaid ID, provider type, group affiliation and effective dates.

Service support

Assessment, plan, session record, supervision, signature and documentation linked to the billed service.

Authorization and coding

Approved period and units, procedure code, modifier, place of service and units billed.

Evidence to locate

  • the complete audit notice and claim worksheet;
  • program enrollment approval, revalidation and termination records;
  • group affiliation and reassignment records where relevant;
  • managed-care credentialing or participation records;
  • authorizations and approved units by period;
  • the original clinical and billing records maintained for each claim; and
  • the cited provider manual, bulletin, contract or policy version.

Questions for attorney review

The organized file should let counsel examine which authority the payer invokes, whether it applied on the date of service, how the payer characterizes billing and rendering providers, whether separate findings are being treated cumulatively, how the demand was calculated and which administrative or contractual response path may apply. The platform presents these as review questions and does not determine the answer.

National source coverage without a false coverage claim

AuditDefend tracks official entry points for all 50 state Medicaid programs and the District of Columbia, together with federal Medicare and other national sources. A listed source means the system has an official monitoring or research entry point—not that every contract, bulletin, manual version or local rule has already been interpreted for the case.
