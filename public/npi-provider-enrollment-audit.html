NPI & PROVIDER ENROLLMENT AUDIT

Enrollment is a timeline—not a yes-or-no field.

A current NPI or current program enrollment does not answer who was enrolled, affiliated or authorized on an earlier date of service. Review the billing entity and rendering individual separately.

Build the timeline →
Enrollment chronology fields
  • billing Type 2 or Type 1 NPI;
  • rendering Type 1 NPI;
  • program-specific provider ID;
  • provider type and taxonomy;
  • group affiliation or reassignment; and
  • effective, revalidation and termination dates.

What should be tested when a payer cites an unenrolled NPI?

Identify exactly which identifier the payer names, which provider role it assigns to that identifier, which program or plan allegedly required enrollment and which dates of service are affected. Then preserve the official or payer source used to establish the enrollment period. Do not substitute a present-day lookup for historical evidence without recording that limitation.

The legal or contractual consequence of an enrollment finding is not inferred from the fact pattern. It remains subject to attorney review of the governing program, contract and procedural record.

Keep four questions separate

Who billed?

The billing NPI and legal entity shown on the claim.

Who rendered?

The individual provider associated with the face-to-face or professional service.

What was required?

The payer, program and rule source applicable to the provider role and date.

When was it effective?

Enrollment, affiliation, revalidation and termination dates compared with each service date.

Evidence that may be relevant

  • NPPES records for identifier and taxonomy context;
  • state or federal program enrollment approval and revalidation notices;
  • managed-care credentialing or participation records;
  • group affiliation, reassignment or roster confirmations;
  • payer portal history, correspondence and effective-date notices;
  • the original claim showing billing and rendering fields; and
  • the exact rule, manual or contract language cited for the date at issue.

Do not merge enrollment and documentation findings

A reviewer may record both an enrollment finding and a clinical-documentation finding on the same claim. The existence of one review track does not automatically prove or disprove the other, and a detailed chart review does not by itself establish the legal consequence of an enrollment defect. AuditDefend preserves both findings, their sources and the evidence needed for counsel to analyze them.

Build a date-of-service matrix

Normalize the identifiers.Record full identifiers securely, using redacted labels in public or demonstration material.
Map provider roles.Separate billing entity, rendering individual, supervising clinician and group affiliation.
Attach effective-date evidence.Preserve the source and retrieval date, and distinguish official records from provider-reported facts.
Flag unresolved periods.Show gaps or conflicting dates without converting them into a legal conclusion.
