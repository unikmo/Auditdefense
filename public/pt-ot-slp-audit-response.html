THERAPY INSURANCE AUDIT

Tie every billed therapy minute back to the plan of care and the note that supports it.

Physical, occupational and speech therapy reviews often turn on timed codes, plans of care, progress reports, certifications and who rendered each visit. Each issue should stay attached to its own claim line, date of service and course of treatment.

Organize the audit →
For each affected line, identify
  • CPT code, modifier and units;
  • date of service and treatment minutes recorded;
  • discipline and rendering clinician or assistant;
  • supervising therapist where applicable;
  • active plan of care and any certification; and
  • enrollment and credentialing periods.

Start with the payer’s exact allegation

“Records do not support the service” can describe different alleged defects: minutes that do not reach the billed units, a visit outside the plan-of-care period, a missing or late certification, an unsigned note, a service rendered by an unrecognized provider type, or an enrollment issue. Preserve the reviewer’s wording and give each allegation its own issue category.

AuditDefend identifies factual gaps and conflicts. It does not decide medical necessity, compliance, repayment liability or the legal sufficiency of a response.

Therapy evidence map

Evaluation and plan of care

Evaluation date, author, goals, frequency and duration, plan period, recertifications and any physician or practitioner certification.

Visit record

Date, timed and untimed codes, minutes recorded, interventions, clinician and signature.

Progress and function

Progress reports, functional measures and the record of why care continued or changed.

Provider and administrative support

Discipline, assistant supervision, authorization, billing and rendering identifiers, credentialing and enrollment effective dates.

Timed-code arithmetic, kept separate

Timed codes depend on total minutes and how a payer converts minutes to units. When a payer recalculates units, keep its method, the minutes it used and the resulting count next to your own record. Do not merge a unit recalculation with a separate documentation or plan-of-care finding on the same claim.

How to verify a worksheet finding

Select the affected claim line.Confirm the member-safe identifier, date of service, code, units and rendering provider.
Preserve the payer’s stated reason.Do not substitute a broader label for the actual line-level decision.
Match the visit to its plan of care.Check that the date of service falls inside a documented plan period and note any certification dates.
Record the comparison.Mark what appears present, absent or inconsistent and reserve conclusions for professional and attorney review.

Why issue separation matters

A claim may have a complete visit note while the payer separately alleges a plan-of-care gap, a medical-necessity concern or an enrollment defect. Evidence that answers one does not answer the others. AuditDefend keeps each track separate so the provider and counsel can see exactly what each record addresses.
